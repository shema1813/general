Facts about dogs

Dogs have an exceptional sense of smell and hearing, a sense of time, and can be right or left-pawed like humans. They also have unique physiological traits like a third eyelid for eye protection and only sweat through their paws, a process aided by panting. Historically, dogs were the first animals domesticated by humans and have evolved to assist in various roles, from hunting to providing companionship today.

Their history

Dogs originated from ancient wolves that were domesticated by humans starting roughly 15,000 to 40,000 years ago, evolving into the first domesticated animal. This process began when less aggressive wolves scavenged near human hunter-gatherer camps for food scraps, and this "survival of the friendliest" led to a co-dependent relationship where they gained food and protection from humans in exchange for being a food source, and later, as working companions.

Did you know?

Strange facts about dogs include that their nose prints are unique like human fingerprints, some dogs can yodel instead of bark, and a dog's nose is sensitive enough to be used as evidence in court. Dalmatians are born white and develop spots later, and a dog's intelligence is comparable to a two-year-old child, with some able to learn over 200 words.
